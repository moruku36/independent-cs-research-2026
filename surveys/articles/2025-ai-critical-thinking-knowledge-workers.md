# Generative AI and critical thinking in knowledge work — CHI 2025 survey note

## Reader summary

In 319 knowledge workers' 936 self-reported examples, the authors find that greater task-specific confidence in AI is associated with less reported critical thinking, while greater confidence in one's own ability is associated with more. They describe a shift toward verifying information, integrating outputs and stewarding the task. A cross-sectional self-report study cannot establish that AI causes a lasting loss of thinking ability.

This note appears under the repository's work and labor collection. The source is a **CHI 2025 academic paper**, not a company article.

## Survey record

- **Status:** Primary PDF checked; methods, results and limitations partially read. No reproduction.
- **Bibliography:** Hao-Ping (Hank) Lee, Advait Sarkar, Lev Tankelevitch, Ian Drosos, Sean Rintel, Richard Banks, Nicholas Wilson, *The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers*, CHI 2025, 23 pages, [DOI:10.1145/3706598.3713778](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778). [Primary PDF hosted by Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf).
- **Checked:** October 23, 2026; linked PDF.
- **Question:** When do GenAI users report critical thinking at work, and how is its effort associated with confidence in AI and in oneself?
- **Method:** Prolific survey of knowledge workers using GenAI at least weekly. Of 333 responses, 14 low-quality responses were excluded; 319 participants and 936 examples were analyzed with Bloom-based self-reports, explanatory regression and qualitative responses.
- **Reported evidence:** Task-specific AI confidence is associated with less reported critical thinking and self-confidence with more. Respondents describe verification, integration and task stewardship. See [§3–6](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf).
- **Limits:** Cross-sectional self-report does not show causality or long-term skill loss. Reduced effort is not identical to reduced thinking; self-confidence is not objective expertise. The sample may favor English-speaking, younger and technically comfortable users. See §6.3.
- **Comparison:** This studies perceived cognition during work, not headcount or measured productivity.
- **Free initial check:** Classify public questionnaire items by whether they measure effort, accuracy or expertise. Not done; a new survey would not by itself reproduce the paper or identify causal effects.
- **Use status:** Proposals for verification and counterevidence steps only; no intervention effect demonstrated.
- **Open checks:** Availability of anonymized data and analysis code; independent coefficient re-estimation.

A work-design question raised by this survey is who verifies outputs, owns errors and keeps skills current. Objective quality measures, non-AI tasks and longitudinal observation would be needed to distinguish saved effort from skill loss. This is our interpretation, not an intervention tested by the paper.
