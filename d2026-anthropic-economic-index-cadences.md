## 2. Executive Summary
This report combines privacy-preserving telemetry across Claude.ai, Claude Desktop (Cowork), Claude Code, and 1P API traffic (sampled between April 10 and June 10, 2026) with responses from the Anthropic Economic Index Survey (~9,700 users, launched in April 2026).
Structured around three pillars—**Cadences** (hourly and weekly usage rhythms), **Artifacts** (concrete deliverables and token economics), and **Perceptions** (how usage shapes economic beliefs)—the report connects observed AI workflows with worker expectations. A central finding is the "optimism paradox": users who rely on Claude in the most automated ways expect AI to take over a larger share of their tasks within the next year, yet remain the most optimistic about positive impacts on their pay, job security, and work meaning.

## 4. Chapter Breakdown

### Chapter 1: Cadences (AI Etched into the Clock)
- **Weekend Ventures vs. Weekday Work**: Personal use rises on weekends (especially in high-income countries). In Claude Code, weekday clusters like backend architecture and API debugging give way on weekends to AI agent design, quant trading, gaming, and entrepreneurship/side-income ideation.
- **Asymmetric Overtime**: When work-related conversations occur on nights and weekends, they skew toward higher-wage occupations (using BLS OEWS wage quartiles), indicating that AI extends working hours primarily for elite knowledge workers rather than clerical or telemarketing roles.

### Chapter 2: Artifacts (What People Actually Take Away)
- **From Q&A to Deliverables**: Classifying conversation outputs reveals that 93% of sessions produce identifiable artifacts (reports, code, plans).
- **Autonomy and Compute Scaling**: Tasks leaving more judgment to Claude (e.g., building a website vs. translating text) require higher autonomy surfaces (like Claude Code) and consume significantly more tokens, linking compute expenditure directly to artifact complexity and economic value.

### Chapter 3: Perceptions (Connecting Usage to Belief)
- **Alignment with Exposure Metrics**: Self-reported task automation correlates positively with both observed usage exposure and theoretical exposure indices (e.g., Eloundou et al.).
- **Skill Valuation**: As automation share rises, users increasingly report that their skills have become *more* valuable, while ~69% consistently report learning more.
- **Demographic Divergence**: While women log more active minutes in Claude, men exhibit higher usage shares of Claude Code and higher rates of automated/agentic delegation.
